# MAT-DSC-001 — DSC signal interpretation and thermal transitions

**Review state:** `candidate` (bibliographic metadata checked for one relevant standard; passages and current edition not audited).
**Knowledge type:** interpretation methodology, not a unique transition-assignment law.

## Observation versus inference

Describe the DSC trace as an **observed heat-flow signal** before assigning glass transition, crystallization, melting, softening or overlapping transitions. A broad or weak endothermic feature is not proof that the specimen underwent full crystalline melting. Check heat-flow direction, reference baseline, enthalpy/sign conventions, heating/cooling cycle and thermal history.

## Conditions for interpretation

- Sample identity/mass; atmospheric conditions; pan type and calibration.
- First heating, controlled cooling and second heating must be identified separately.
- Onset/peak/endset definitions must be linked to the actual measurement method and reference baseline.
- If degradation, moisture loss, relaxation or multiple phases are plausible, record alternative explanations rather than overclaiming a single mechanism.

## Separating data sources

Keep DSC-observed features separate from thermal-model predictions or IR thermal images. A predicted peak surface temperature cannot by itself establish the same transition that would be measured by DSC.
