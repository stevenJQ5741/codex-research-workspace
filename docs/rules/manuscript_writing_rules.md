# Manuscript Writing Rules

## Purpose

Rules for academic English manuscripts, abstracts, reports, figure captions, and discussion sections.

## Core principles

1. Be precise.
2. Avoid overclaiming.
3. Preserve the user's intended logic unless asked to restructure.
4. Separate results, assumptions, interpretation, and speculation.
5. Do not invent citations or data.
6. Keep paragraphs connected to surrounding sections.
7. Provide directly usable replacement text when revising manuscripts.

## Preferred expressions

Use:

- suggests
- indicates
- is consistent with
- may be attributed to
- can be interpreted as
- within the assumptions of the model
- under the present experimental conditions

Avoid unsupported:

- proves
- completely confirms
- fully demonstrates
- solely caused by
- universally demonstrates

## Scientific caution

Do not write:

> This proves that the interface was improved.

Prefer:

> This result suggests that the interfacial condition was improved under the present processing conditions.

Do not write:

> The model proves that melting occurred.

Prefer:

> The model supports the possibility that the surface entered a high-softening or partial-melting temperature range.

## Revision format

When revising text, usually provide:

1. final replacement paragraph
2. short note on the main change if useful
3. no unnecessary alternatives unless requested
