# Natural Fiber Project Rules

## Purpose

Rules for natural-fiber composite projects, e-ASIA, China-Japan-Malaysia collaboration, PALF/PBS, PLA, and agricultural validation.

## e-ASIA / tri-country logic

Main concept:

- Standardized natural fiber preparation.
- Composite material system development.
- Practical field validation and feedback.

Work split:

- China: upstream fiber preparation, laser-assisted pretreatment, mechanical separation, cleaning, standardized fiber library.
- Japan: midstream composite design, interface analysis, thermal and mechanical evaluation, material and structure design.
- Malaysia: downstream IoT monitoring, image recognition, fuzzy logic, field validation, and performance feedback.

## Core rules

1. Treat PALF as an example, not the only target fiber.
2. Keep the scope broader than one fiber type.
3. Emphasize standardization, feedback, and closed-loop optimization.
4. Do not make the project sound like only material testing.
5. Preserve the tri-country structure.
6. Emphasize Japan's unique role in material design, interface evaluation, thermal/mechanical testing, and model-based feedback.

## PALF/PBS low-thermal-conductivity project

Rules:

1. Distinguish two-phase and three-phase models.
2. Discuss fiber, matrix, and interphase separately.
3. Do not ignore voids or density effects.
4. Connect thermal conductivity reduction to both material and structure.
5. Include mechanical-property trade-offs where relevant.

## Short-fiber shape-change project

Rules:

1. Connect response behavior to microstructure.
2. Include orientation, length degradation, voids, interface, and network effects.
3. Discuss response speed, recovery ratio, recovery force, and durability.
4. Avoid claiming complete novelty unless literature supports it.

## Flame-retardant or heat-resistant 3D printing project

Rules:

1. Distinguish heat resistance, dimensional stability, residual strength, and flame retardancy.
2. Do not overstate flame retardancy without standardized flame tests.
3. Report deformation, delamination, residual strength, and dimensional retention separately.
