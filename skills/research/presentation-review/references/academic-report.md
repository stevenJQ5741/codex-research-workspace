# Academic and group-meeting mode

Use for scientific meetings, lab reports, conference research talks and research
planning. Adapt to audience and talk time. A natural route is research object and
motivation → existing evidence and limits → question or hypothesis → method and
independent validation → results or planned evidence → discussion and next action.
This is a choice of narrative, not a required slide count or fixed page order.

For each page, identify its scientific role and distinguish measurements,
calculations, literature and interpretations. Explain what evidence supports,
what remains uncertain and which alternative explanations matter. When planning
experiments, relate hypotheses to controls, observable outcomes and possible
disconfirmation; do not imply proposed findings are measured results.

Starting targets from the user's academic style: section 40 pt, slide subject
24 pt, module 18 pt, body 16 pt, figure annotation 13–14 pt and citations about
12 pt. Fit to actual screen, language, visual evidence and user instructions.
Use the largest legible original research figures, tables or diagrams consistent
with the page's argument. A discussion page may end with open decisions instead
of a conventional thank-you slide when appropriate.

By default show complete per-slide references near the evidence, including
authors, full title, publication, year, volume/pages or article number and DOI
when available; retain full verified details in the source ledger. Allocate the
footnote area before fixing the research-image area. Follow explicit venue or
user citation requirements if they differ.
