# Personal research-presentation style and evidence

Use this profile for Jiang's research presentations when its scope is selected in
the entrypoint. These are reusable personal defaults, not claims about every prior
slide. Explicit current instructions, an approved baseline and venue rules control
over the defaults. Preserve project-specific facts and assets in the project.

## Visual grammar

- Favor a white or very light background, dark-blue section labels, short gray
  horizontal bands, blue module headings and process arrows, black explanation,
  and concise red emphasis for important evidence, limits or decisions.
- A section label provides navigation; a separate slide title identifies the
  scientific subject or supported takeaway. Use real research images as evidence;
  text explains their scientific role. Organize substantial information with
  clear groups and reading order without shrinking labels below readability.
- Start with 16:9 and, when PNG previews are requested, 1920×1080 pixels. Treat
  placement, color samples and font sizes as adaptable starting points. Pixel sizes
  and PowerPoint points are different: calibrate through actual rendering.
- Use the correct organization's official, high-quality mark if available; preserve
  its aspect ratio and consistent positioning. Never invent or redraw a logo, and
  do not assume a previous project's institution applies to this one.
- Design cover separately from content pages. Avoid commercial card grids,
  decorative visuals, unrelated arrows, and busy styling that obscures evidence.
  Use arrows only for genuine process, temporal or input-output relationships.

## Evidence and citations

- First choose the slide's purpose, then select relevant original figures,
  photographs, data and captions. Distinguish own measured work, external evidence,
  calculations, hypotheses, proposed plans and scenario projections; separately
  record whether each source and claim is verified, pending or unavailable.
- Preserve material identity, specimen, conditions, axes, units, legends, error
  bars and scale bars. Trace each original figure to its actual source and figure
  number. A related paper is not automatically the image's publishing source.
- Use conceptual diagrams only as labeled illustrations or hypotheses. Never
  depict invented microscopy, curves, data, statistical significance, experiments,
  credentials, partnerships, equipment availability or market results as facts.
- Keep numbers attached to their year, region, denominator, units and category
  where material. Distinguish actual output, capacity, plan, revenue, profit and
  scenario. Compare processes only on compatible system and quality boundaries;
  distinguish measured amounts from ratios, assumptions and derived estimates.
- Maintain one source ledger and per-slide claim/figure mapping. Number citations
  in order of first appearance for each new deck; repeat the same number for the
  same source, and recheck globally when slide order or sources change. Apply the
  selected mode's on-slide citation format; retain complete metadata and links in
  the internal ledger. Cite only sources actually checked and disclose gaps.
- Use JIF only when relevant to the task or requested; verify the applicable JCR
  year and metric and mark unavailable values pending. JIF is not proof of the
  quality or novelty of an individual result.

## Workflow and QA

Preserve approved source, slide scope and frozen content. Track content, visual
style and output versions separately when their distinction matters. Build the
scientific story and evidence mapping before detailed layout. Reserve citation
space early; show meaningful uncertainty and limitations. Use a representative
sample when iterative style calibration is requested, then follow the user's
authorized batch scope. Do not impose a separate approval at every stage on a
complete deliverable already requested.

Inspect rendered slides at the scale needed for legibility, crops, axes, logo,
overlap and cited text. For requested 1920×1080 PNG, check actual image dimensions;
upscaling a lower-resolution render does not prove native detail. Existing PPTX
revision audits and native Office release are governed by the parent skill.
Report technical QA separately from subjective user approval. An approved visual
style or frozen image does not establish independent factual verification.
