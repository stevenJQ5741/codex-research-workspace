# Research assessment and interview mode

Use for talent recruitment, research-project evaluation, research-plan interviews
and similar decision-oriented presentations. Confirm or infer from the materials
the formal assessment purpose, audience, allowed time, relevant application and
requested deliverable. A missing notification is not a confirmed time limit.

Map the active application or task document to the proposed claims, objectives,
products, milestones, promised resources and evaluation criteria. Distinguish
binding commitments from implementation detail and clearly flag proposed scope
extensions. Never silently change the submitted targets or imply an unconfirmed
platform resource, partnership, qualification, result or business order.

Build a decision path that suits the actual call: applicant and verified track
record → need and opportunity → technical constraint → testable approach →
execution, milestones and outputs → applicant-platform complementarity. Show
which prior work makes each task feasible, while keeping the applicant's actual
background visible. Each page advances a reviewer judgment and connects to the
next; 2–3 related evidence groups is a starting point, not a mandatory quota.
Allocate speaking time before selecting visuals; protect the explanation of the
technical plan and deliverables. Backup pages may hold detailed derivations,
source data, assumptions, expected questions and alternatives.

Use positive, precise professional language calibrated to the evidence. Show
planned and speculative outcomes as such. Evaluate innovation by the concrete,
measurable distinction and validation plan, not by promotional adjectives. Show
economic scenario formulae and assumptions; do not equate a projection with a
signed order, audited revenue, profit or a formal project commitment.

Starting targets from the user's interview style: section 40 pt, page number
16 pt, slide title 28 pt, body and module heading 20 pt, main equation 26 pt,
and citations about 12 pt. Omit numeric prefixes on section labels unless the user
asks; normal slide numbers remain. Give the cover and closing slide a restrained
formal layout. These sizes are starting points, not a guarantee of readability.

Default on-slide journal reference: `[n] first author et al., official journal
abbreviation, volume(issue), pages/article number, year.` Shorten reports and
web sources appropriately; keep full title, DOI/URL, publication date, figure
location and claim mapping in the ledger. Use another format when explicitly
requested or required by the venue. Verify numbering after every source or page
change.

For a substantial task, a page card can capture reviewer question, one takeaway,
application mapping, planned speaking time, original figure and evidence status,
on-slide text, citation, transition, likely question and supporting backup. Keep
design notes and internal review records out of the audience-facing slide.
