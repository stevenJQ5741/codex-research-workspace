# Style, Evidence, and Language Baseline

## Template-derived visual identity

Derive colors, typography, institutional identity, page chrome, and spacing from
the current user-approved deck or supplied template. Do not encode one project's
palette, logo position, or page geometry as a universal rule.

Across templates:

- preserve consistent alignment and usable whitespace
- keep one dominant message per slide
- keep citations readable near sourced literature figures
- reserve strong emphasis for the main warning, limitation, or takeaway
- avoid decorative complexity that competes with evidence

Preserve the active template unless redesign is authorized.

## Slide-title rule

Prefer a message title over a topic label.

- Weak: `Results`
- Better: `Automatic values are lower and show more variation`
- Weak: `Method`
- Better: `A fixed rule finds the three points`

Use a topic title only when a visible takeaway supplies the message elsewhere on the slide.

## Evidence-form decision table

| Audience need | Preferred form | Required check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| See a trend or sensitivity | Chart | axes, units, series identity, readable labels |
| Read exact values or conditions | Table | source, units, precision, statistic definition |
| See the specimen or setup | Photo | honest crop, scale or orientation when needed |
| Understand geometry or mechanism | Schematic | labels match terminology and claim boundary |
| Follow a calculation | Equation plus defined symbols | formula provenance, units, derived versus measured values |
| Defend a literature statement | Source figure or concise redraw | citation, readable labels, no misleading crop |

Reject a visual that only fills space. A visual must prove, explain, compare, operationalize, or defend the slide message.

## Common slide archetypes

### Motivation bridge

Connect the engineering problem to the quantity being evaluated. End with a short need statement.

### Gap and purpose

Show the existing practice, unresolved validation problem, and what this study tests or develops. Avoid implying the method is already validated.

### Method logic

Combine a simplified schematic or representative curve with short definitions. Keep measured values, detected points, and calculated outputs visually distinct.

### Result comparison

Use a chart for the dominant trend and a small table or callout for exact differences. State which statistic is used.

### Failure-mode diagnosis

Show the overall curve and an enlarged region, then separate curve feature, effect on selection, and possible cause. Do not present a possible cause as demonstrated.

### Conclusion

Use three or four parallel statements: demonstrated scope, main result, limitation, and next step. Avoid introducing new evidence.

### Backup evidence

Use exact values, derivations, sensitivity checks, full-resolution images, or proposed validation logic. Keep backup slides presentation-ready and clearly marked.

## Academic English on slides

- Prefer compact noun phrases and short declarative sentences.
- Use consistent project terminology even when repetition feels stylistically plain.
- Use `supports`, `indicates`, `suggests`, `may`, and `proposed` to calibrate limited evidence.
- Avoid promotional language such as `breakthrough`, `fully accurate`, or `universal` unless directly supported.
- Define abbreviations at first meaningful use.
- Keep units attached to axes, table headers, or quantities.

## Simple oral-English notes

Write notes for speech, not for publication.

1. Start with the point of the slide.
2. Direct attention to the relevant figure region or comparison.
3. State the result in one short sentence.
4. Separate interpretation from observation.
5. End with the reason for the next slide.

Default measurable gates for a user requesting simple oral English:

- main-talk average sentence length: at most 10 words
- main-talk maximum sentence length: at most 18 words
- no missing notes on content slides
- no notes on a deliberate divider slide unless the user wants a transition
- project difficult-word list absent, except approved technical terms

Do not split a sentence so aggressively that pronouns lose their referents or scientific relationships become ambiguous.

## Multilingual adaptation

### English conference mode

Compress the visible text, keep claims cautious, and place explanation in the notes.

### Japanese lecture or internal mode

Allow more explanatory scaffolding and explicit process statements. Keep the final lesson or interpretation visually distinct.

### Chinese planning or internal mode

Make logic and decision points explicit, but keep final slide text formal and technical.

Never translate line by line. Rebuild the message for the target audience while preserving the same evidence boundary.

## Scientific claim labels

Use these labels in the revision ledger when risk is high:

- `E`: direct experimental result
- `C`: calculation from identified inputs
- `L`: literature-supported statement
- `I`: interpretation consistent with evidence
- `A`: assumption or methodological convention
- `P`: proposed mechanism, check, or future method

A slide may contain several labels. Its title must not imply a stronger category than the underlying evidence.
